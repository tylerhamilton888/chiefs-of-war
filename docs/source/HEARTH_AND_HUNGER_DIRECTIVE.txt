# HEARTH AND HUNGER
## Complete Project Pivot & Development Directive
### Godot 4 | Existing GitHub Repository | 2D Top-Down Fantasy Sandbox

You are continuing development of our existing Godot 4 game, formerly known as **Chiefs of War**.

We are making a major creative pivot.

The new working title is:

# HEARTH AND HUNGER
*A Nomadic Ogre Sandbox RPG*

This is a fundamental transformation of our game's identity, characters, worldbuilding, and progression systems.

However, it is **not permission to carelessly abandon or destroy the work already completed**.

Inspect our existing Godot project and GitHub repository, identify reusable systems, preserve our development history, and adapt the game to this new vision.

Our objectives are to create a commercially viable, genuinely enjoyable game with exceptional moment-to-moment gameplay, a distinctive identity, and meaningful long-term progression.

**Do actual implementation work. Do not stop at writing design documents or producing concept art.**

---

# 1. THE CENTRAL FANTASY

Hearth and Hunger is a 2D top-down, hand-drawn fantasy sandbox in which the player lives as an enormous, immensely powerful Ogre belonging to a diminished, once-dominant civilization.

Our game combines:

- Satisfying gathering, building, crafting, and daily life.
- Tight, responsive action RPG combat.
- Brutal physical strength and battlefield dominance.
- Hunting and consuming enormous fantasy creatures.
- Taming, breeding, training, and riding great beasts.
- Establishing and upgrading a traveling household.
- Building a growing clan of followers and family members.
- Mercenary contracts, raiding, conquest, and diplomacy.
- Relationships, traditions, religion, and dynastic legacy.
- A freely explorable and reactive fantasy world.

Our original description, **"Stardew Valley for the Boys,"** remains useful, but our distinctive identity has evolved.

We are building a game where a player can be an Ogre hunter, mercenary, craftsman, beastmaster, husband, father, patriarch, chieftain, or conqueror.

These are not restrictive classes.

The player remains an individual person who participates directly in the world.

**The game should be enjoyable whether the player controls one Ogre with a favorite hunting beast or a legendary patriarch commanding a traveling civilization.**

The goal is a compelling "one more day" gameplay loop in which personal accomplishments produce permanent and visible changes.

---

# 2. THE DEFINING MECHANIC: THE WANDERING HEARTH

Our civilization is fundamentally nomadic.

Unlike traditional settlement-building games, the player's home and community should be capable of traveling alongside him.

The player might begin with a crude sled or wagon and a small collection of possessions.

Eventually, he could command a vast traveling household containing:

- Great beast-drawn wagons.
- Family members and retainers.
- Ogre warriors and hunters.
- Cooks, craftsmen, and laborers.
- Lesser servants and thralls.
- Portable workshops.
- Animal breeding facilities.
- Storage wagons.
- Sacred religious objects.
- Hunting trophies.
- Enormous cooking fires and feast halls.

The caravan must eventually exist physically within the game world.

The player should be able to walk among its inhabitants and interact with its activities.

## Deploying camp

When the caravan stops, the player can establish a temporary settlement.

Wagons are parked, shelters erected, cooking stations prepared, beasts secured, and workshops made available.

The player can place or customize structures and arrange the camp according to his preferences.

Eventually, saved camp layouts and autonomous labor should make deployment efficient.

## Breaking camp

When the player decides to move, the clan packs up.

Do not require the player to manually dismantle every object.

Packing and deployment should become satisfying visual processes supported by automation.

The caravan can then travel to new hunting grounds, settlements, mercenary contracts, or unexplored regions.

**The player should feel like he is traveling with his civilization rather than abandoning his home whenever he goes adventuring.**

## Permanent holdings

Players may also establish permanent settlements.

They might appoint wives, adult sons, captains, or trusted retainers to oversee these holdings.

Permanent settlements can provide food, equipment, trade, recruits, tribute, and breeding grounds.

However, the player should not become trapped in administrative menus.

**The player is a roaming Ogre first and a ruler second.**

Growing political power should increase freedom and opportunity rather than administrative busywork.

---

# 3. OGRES — PHYSICAL AND CULTURAL IDENTITY

Our playable people are now **Ogres, not Orcs**.

This distinction is extremely important.

Do not simply recolor the existing Orc models or scale them up.

Ogres should feel like an entirely different species.

## Male Ogre anatomy

Use the immense strongman physique of Thor from God of War Ragnarök as a general anatomical reference, without copying the character's design.

The ideal male Ogre has:

- Tremendous height and physical mass.
- Immensely broad shoulders.
- Powerful arms and hands.
- Thick neck and barrel chest.
- Strong, heavy torso.
- Substantial but proportionate legs.
- Powerful jaws and prominent brow ridges.
- Structured, broad noses.
- Distinctive lower tusks or protruding canine teeth.
- Nonhuman proportions that remain physically believable.

Do not make every male Ogre excessively obese.

The standard playable Ogre should look like an enormous, powerful heavyweight strongman.

Some Ogres may be heavier, others more muscular or rugged, but none should look like an ordinary human.

## Female Ogre anatomy

Female Ogres should be unmistakably members of the same species.

However, preserve traditionally feminine attractiveness.

Our female design direction emphasizes:

- Beautiful, expressive faces.
- Feminine features.
- Voluptuous but believable proportions.
- Strong bodies without excessive masculine musculature.
- Broader Ogreish facial structure.
- Modest but recognizable tusks.
- Thick, beautiful hair.
- Nordic-inspired braids and ornaments.
- Strong hips, legs, and shoulders appropriate to their size.

Do not make all female Ogres resemble bodybuilders.

They should be imposing, physically capable, and visually attractive while remaining recognizably nonhuman.

The current plan is **male-only playable characters for the initial release**, with female Ogres serving as important NPCs.

## Culture

Ogres are fiercely patriarchal, traditional, proud, and devoted to their own people.

Their values emphasize:

**Strength. Blood. Honor. Tradition. Hunger.**

They consider themselves naturally superior to smaller peoples because they generally are physically superior.

They are loyal to their households, clans, and traditions, while often remaining indifferent to the welfare of outsiders.

Some lesser peoples serve them willingly, others through necessity or coercion.

Ogres are capable of affection, loyalty, family life, humor, and generosity, but their culture should remain strange and savage by ordinary human standards.

Avoid presenting them as universally evil villains or secretly modern moralizing heroes.

Allow individuals to vary substantially.

---

# 4. THE FALLEN OGRE CIVILIZATION

Ogres were once the dominant people across large parts of the world.

Their ancient power rested upon their physical strength, hunting traditions, mighty beasts, and enormous wandering clans.

Something caused their civilization to decline.

Potential factors include:

- Catastrophic environmental changes.
- The disappearance of enormous migratory herds.
- Civil wars and tribal division.
- Loss of ancestral breeding knowledge.
- Encroachment by more numerous peoples.
- A mysterious ancient catastrophe.

Relatively few Ogres remain.

However, they are still individually among the world's strongest intelligent inhabitants.

A single Ogre is a formidable combatant.

Ten Ogres constitute a serious military force.

Twenty trained Ogres are enough to threaten a substantial settlement.

Their enemies must rely upon numbers, discipline, technology, traps, or exceptional monsters.

This disparity is a major part of the gameplay identity.

The player may seek to restore his people's former supremacy, but that ambition is optional.

He may instead pursue wealth, adventure, family life, or mercenary service.

---

# 5. COMBAT — OUR HIGHEST GAMEPLAY PRIORITY

Combat must become one of Hearth and Hunger's greatest attractions.

Take inspiration from the fluidity, impact, and multi-enemy combat satisfaction of Shadow of War, while building a completely original system suited to top-down 2D gameplay.

**Do not produce a slow, clumsy combat system simply because Ogres are enormous.**

Ogres should feel devastatingly powerful but responsive.

Their physical mass should influence how they attack, defend, grapple, and interact with enemies.

## Combat fundamentals

Implement and refine:

- Precise movement and facing.
- Responsive light and heavy attacks.
- Directional melee attacks.
- Proper blocking.
- Perfect parries and counterattacks.
- Dodge rolls or powerful evasive steps.
- Attack chaining and combos.
- Guard breaks.
- Staggering and knockback.
- Interruptions and recovery.
- Reliable hitboxes.
- Clear attack telegraphs.
- Skillful positioning.
- Distinctive weapon behaviors.
- Appropriate audio and visual impact feedback.

Controls should initially support WASD movement, mouse aiming, mouse attacks and defense, Space for dodging, and configurable interaction keys.

Provide input buffering and carefully tuned cancel windows.

The player should feel in control during combat.

## The Ogre power fantasy

This is critical.

**Against ordinary opponents, the player should usually be the most physically dangerous creature on the battlefield.**

An Ogre should not exchange weak, inconsequential blows with individual goblins.

He should be capable of knocking smaller enemies aside, smashing their defenses, and overpowering them.

However, smaller enemies become dangerous through numbers, tactics, positioning, ranged attacks, and specialized equipment.

A group of disciplined spearmen should be much more dangerous than a single spearman.

A pack of goblins should attempt to surround, harass, distract, and exhaust the player.

Large monsters and rival Ogres should create more direct physical challenges.

## Creative combat mechanics

Explore the following original combat ideas.

### Mighty Blows

Powerful attacks that can knock lesser enemies backward, interrupt their actions, break weak shields, and damage destructible obstacles.

### Grappling and Throwing

Allow Ogres to seize smaller enemies, throw them aside, slam them down, or use them to disrupt nearby foes.

These attacks must use proper hand-drawn animation frames and accurate collision behavior.

Large enemies should resist or be immune to inappropriate grappling techniques.

### Battlefield Objects

Allow the player to interact physically with certain environmental objects.

Examples:

- Break wooden barricades.
- Smash flimsy doors.
- Kick smaller enemies into obstacles.
- Lift or throw appropriately sized objects.
- Charge through light cover.
- Use oversized improvised weapons.

Do not make the environment universally destructible at the cost of performance or scope.

Prioritize a few highly satisfying interactions.

### Overpower and Counter

A properly timed parry or guard break can create an opportunity for a devastating counterattack.

The counter should feel like a powerful Ogre exploiting an enemy's mistake.

### Ferocity

Experiment with a momentum mechanic that rewards skilled aggression.

Successful attacks, counters, parries, and other accomplishments may build combat momentum.

This can unlock powerful attacks, brief bursts of strength, or special finishing moves.

Avoid allowing endless button-mashing to generate the same rewards as skilled play.

---

# 6. EATING AND FEEDING AS COMBAT MECHANICS

Eating is a major part of Ogre biology and culture.

It must become central to gameplay.

## Battle Feeding

Introduce contextual feeding finishers.

An Ogre may overpower a staggered or critically wounded enemy and perform a brief, brutal feeding animation.

Potential rewards include:

- Recovering health.
- Restoring Ferocity.
- Temporary strength or resistance bonuses.
- Intimidating nearby enemies.
- Gaining special benefits from unusual creatures.

These should be quick, responsive, and integrated into combat.

Use stylized animation rather than excessive graphic detail.

## Executions

Support contextual brutal finishers following guard breaks, staggers, critical strikes, or successful counters.

Occasional stylized dismemberment of smaller opponents is appropriate.

Executions should communicate tremendous physical power without becoming gratuitous or repetitive.

Different weapons should eventually have distinctive finishers.

Do not interrupt combat with lengthy unskippable cinematic sequences.

Feeding finishers must be an option, not something required for every enemy.

## Eating and preparation

Food consumed outside combat should also matter.

Meat from different creatures can provide useful bonuses.

Examples:

- Endurance improvements.
- Recovery benefits.
- Temporary resistance to stagger.
- Strength bonuses.
- Special effects associated with magical or monstrous creatures.

Avoid an oppressive survival hunger meter.

A well-fed Ogre should be meaningfully rewarded, but ordinary activities should remain enjoyable without constant eating interruptions.

---

# 7. ENEMY DESIGN AND COMBAT ENCOUNTERS

Our enemies must be mechanically and visually distinct.

## Goblins

Small, individually weak, cunning, and numerous.

They use ranged weapons, traps, ambushes, and coordinated attacks.

They should be vulnerable to the player's tremendous strength.

## Orcs

Powerful tribal fighters who are generally smaller than Ogres.

They use shields, axes, spears, and organized warbands.

Experienced Orc champions may present serious challenges through superior technique and equipment.

## Humans and other smaller civilizations

Humans generally cannot match Ogre physical strength.

Their advantages include disciplined formations, ranged weapons, fortifications, cavalry, and superior numbers.

## Rival Ogres

Rival Ogres are among the most dangerous humanoid opponents.

Their fights emphasize timing, strength, grappling, heavy blows, and tactical positioning.

Ogre duels should be especially memorable.

## Trolls and giants

Larger or unusually powerful creatures with distinct anatomy and combat patterns.

## Fantasy megafauna

Enormous beasts that require skill, preparation, and sometimes coordinated hunting parties.

These encounters should involve distinctive attack patterns, weaknesses, and movement.

## Three required combat quality tests

Develop and polish combat around:

**A. One Ogre against several goblins.**

Tests crowd combat, overpowering smaller opponents, and responsiveness.

**B. Ogre versus Ogre champion.**

Tests dueling, blocking, parrying, counters, and precise timing.

**C. Ogre versus enormous monster.**

Tests boss mechanics, positioning, evasion, and large-target combat.

These three encounters must become genuinely enjoyable before expanding into a large enemy roster.

---

# 8. HAND-DRAWN ANIMATION — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Our game uses original, hand-drawn, frame-by-frame 2D character animations.

No awkward skeletal deformation in place of proper full-body animation.

No floating axes or disconnected hands.

No lazy paper-doll character movement.

Every supported action should use coherent illustrated poses with appropriate timing.

Characters must maintain consistent anatomy, hair, equipment, and appearance across animation frames.

Required foundational animations include:

- Idle.
- Walk.
- Run.
- Turn.
- Light attack.
- Heavy attack.
- Block.
- Parry.
- Dodge.
- Stagger.
- Knockback.
- Death.
- Woodcutting.
- Mining.
- Carrying.
- Construction.
- Eating.
- Feeding finishers.
- Grappling.

Do not attempt to finish the entire animation library simultaneously.

Start with a small number of complete, polished actions.

**A properly illustrated Ogre hitting one goblin convincingly is better than twenty unfinished combat abilities.**

The player must remain recognizably the same individual throughout every animation.

---

# 9. HUNTING, GREAT BEASTS, AND ANIMAL HUSBANDRY

Animal husbandry is another foundational system.

Ogres have developed intimate relationships with enormous, dangerous beasts.

These creatures support transportation, combat, hunting, construction, and the mobility of their civilization.

## Great beast roles

Develop original creatures suitable for:

- Pulling enormous caravan wagons.
- Carrying heavy equipment.
- Mounted transportation.
- Hunting alongside Ogres.
- Breaching fortifications.
- Clearing obstacles.
- Traversing difficult terrain.
- Providing useful materials.

Some creatures may consume unusual resources such as mineral-rich stone or vegetation rather than meat.

This should create interesting symbiotic relationships.

For example, a mineral-feeding beast could clear rubble or break certain fortifications while consuming useful material.

Avoid reproducing Warhammer's Stonehorn design or other proprietary creatures.

## Taming

Develop the foundation for animals that can be encountered, tamed, trained, and bonded with.

Animals should have individual identities, strengths, and temperaments.

## Breeding

Eventually support selective breeding for traits such as:

- Physical strength.
- Endurance.
- Movement speed.
- Temperament.
- Carrying capacity.
- Combat suitability.

Breeding should provide meaningful long-term progression without becoming an excessively complicated genetic simulator.

## Mounted combat

Plan for mounted combat as an important future feature.

The player should eventually be able to ride appropriate beasts, fight from them, and participate in monster hunts or military campaigns.

Do not implement elaborate mounted combat before the underlying on-foot combat and animation systems are excellent.

---

# 10. THE HUNTING AND PROSPERITY LOOP

Our hunting system should be more involved than killing an enemy and receiving an item.

The player should eventually be able to:

1. Find tracks or evidence of an animal.
2. Locate or stalk prey.
3. Engage it in combat.
4. Defeat or capture it.
5. Harvest meat, hides, bones, horns, and other resources.
6. Transport valuable materials home.
7. Process food and crafting resources.
8. Use those resources to strengthen the household.

An enormous beast should provide a meaningful quantity of food and valuable materials.

Hunting accomplishments should influence reputation and opportunities.

A legendary hunt could result in a clan feast, impressive trophies, new equipment, and increased standing.

**Hunting, eating, crafting, clan prosperity, and combat progression should reinforce one another.**

---

# 11. FEASTS, FAMILY, AND THE GREAT HEARTH

The domestic side of the game should be satisfying, warm, and culturally distinct.

Our Ogres are capable of strong affection and loyalty toward their own people.

Their relationships are not necessarily gentle, but they are meaningful.

The player may eventually establish a household with spouses, children, relatives, and retainers.

Family members should have personalities and useful roles.

## Feasting

Feasts are important cultural and religious events.

They may celebrate:

- Successful hunts.
- Victories.
- Marriages.
- Births.
- Seasonal events.
- Diplomatic gatherings.
- Clan achievements.

The player should be able to contribute food and resources to a feast.

Feasts may improve relationships, morale, reputation, and clan cohesion.

A great feast should be visually impressive.

The prosperity of the household should be obvious from the amount and quality of food available.

## Dynastic progression

Eventually support inheritance, family relationships, and the possibility of appointing descendants to positions of responsibility.

A powerful Ogre patriarch might establish multiple households and bloodlines.

Family development must not become mandatory for players who prefer a wandering adventurer lifestyle.

---

# 12. RELIGION — THE GODS OF THE FIRST HEARTH

Our Ogre religion is inspired by ancient Norse mythology.

The Ogres worship beings broadly analogous to the Æsir and related mythological figures, but preserve different names, stories, and cultural interpretations.

Provisional deities include:

- **Rauth:** Thunder, physical strength, protection, and victory.
- **Sava:** Hearths, families, abundance, and household prosperity.
- **Veydr:** Wisdom, journeys, leadership, and sacrifice.
- **Karn:** Oaths, courage, and sworn obligations.
- **Skelda:** Hunting, winter, wilderness, and endurance.
- **Velk:** Trickery, audacity, disorder, and opportunity.

These are working names.

Religion should be represented through meaningful physical practices:

- Offerings at the hearth.
- Clan feasts.
- Ancestral shrines.
- Religious banners.
- Sacred wagons.
- Oath ceremonies.
- Hunting rituals.
- Seasonal observances.

The central sacred hearth should travel with the clan.

**Religion must feel integrated into the ordinary lives of Ogres, not simply represented by a passive statistics menu.**

Players may be devout, skeptical, or indifferent.

The actual existence and intervention of the gods may remain mysterious.

---

# 13. MERCENARY WORK, CONQUEST, AND THE LIVING WORLD

Ogres are individually among the strongest peoples in the world.

Other civilizations hire them precisely because they are exceptionally dangerous.

Mercenary contracts should eventually include:

- Monster hunting.
- Caravan protection.
- Military campaigns.
- Siege assaults.
- Frontier defense.
- Bandit suppression.
- Dangerous expeditions.

Employers may pay with money, meat, livestock, equipment, property rights, or access to territory.

The player can develop a reputation for reliability, brutality, greed, honor, or treachery according to his actions.

## Territorial control

The player can eventually conquer or acquire permanent holdings and delegate them to trusted NPCs.

Do not force the player to sit in menus administering these holdings.

Appointed governors should manage ordinary affairs according to broad instructions.

They may send tribute, recruit warriors, develop industries, or maintain breeding herds.

Personal relationships and loyalties should matter.

## NPC simulation

Important NPCs should have persistent identities, skills, relationships, ambitions, and memories.

The world should continue functioning while the player travels.

Develop an original event-driven social simulation rather than copying another game's proprietary NPC hierarchy system.

---

# 14. THE OPENING — A NEW OGRE LIFE

Adapt our established narrative opening to the Ogre setting.

The player begins as a young Ogre belonging to a relatively small nomadic clan.

He learns gathering, hunting, crafting, and combat while participating in ordinary clan life.

He meets a handful of memorable characters.

He joins an expedition against dangerous wildlife or a hostile group.

When he returns, he discovers his clan has been attacked by a larger, stronger Ogre host.

He fights to defend his people.

The invading chieftain notices his courage and challenges him.

The player is defeated by the much more experienced chieftain.

Instead of killing him, the conqueror recognizes his potential and offers him a future within his traveling clan.

The player may eventually:

- Become loyal to the new clan.
- Serve as a hunter or mercenary.
- Become a respected warrior.
- Establish a household.
- Challenge the chieftain.
- Seek revenge.
- Leave and form his own clan.
- Attempt to restore the old Ogre civilization.

The intended defeat should not involve dishonest combat rules or disregard valid player actions. Accommodate exceptional outcomes coherently.

**Do not prioritize building the full narrative prologue until the gameplay systems are enjoyable.**

---

# 15. ART DIRECTION

Our visual target is a colorful, hand-drawn, three-quarter top-down 2D game.

Use the previously approved illustrated Ogre campsite reference as our closest gameplay-art direction.

The world should be vivid, readable, textured only where necessary, and stylistically cohesive.

Our cultural inspiration is Nordic, Germanic, and Celtic, adapted to a nomadic Ogre civilization.

## Visual requirements

- Clearly illustrated character sprites.
- Strong silhouettes.
- Adult, correctly proportioned characters.
- Attractive environments.
- Readable terrain and buildings.
- Natural environmental variation.
- Hand-sewn, imperfect clan banners.
- Huge beast-drawn wagons.
- Portable halls and shelters.
- Carved timber, fur, bone, hide, and heavy iron.
- Believable interactions between characters and equipment.

Avoid:

- Photorealistic or CGI rendering.
- Overly noisy AI-looking detail.
- Disney-like softness.
- Anime aesthetics.
- Chibi proportions.
- Floating weapons or objects.
- Repetitive decorative symbols.
- Factory-perfect banners.
- Copying Warcraft or Warhammer heraldry.
- Permanent stone towns as the default Ogre settlement.

**Nomadism should be immediately recognizable from the environment.**

Ogre homes should ordinarily look capable of being packed up and transported.

Permanent strongholds may exist, but they are not the visual foundation of the civilization.

Use original, commercially usable assets and document their provenance.

If the approved images aren't available to this Work session, request the reference files or proceed with a narrowly scoped visual test based on this written direction.

# 16. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER

We need to avoid producing a vast collection of unfinished systems.

Work through focused milestones.

## Milestone 0 — Safe Project Pivot

Inspect the existing Chiefs of War GitHub repository.

Create a verified backup checkpoint or archival branch before modifying the project.

Preserve reusable systems such as movement, combat, inventory, saving, and interactions.

Update the game title and documentation to Hearth and Hunger.

Retain repository history.

If renaming the remote repository is supported and safe, migrate it appropriately; otherwise keep the existing repository name temporarily while updating the project identity.

Do not destroy working systems or claim changes are published until the remote repository has been verified.

## Milestone 1 — The Living Ogre

Create an excellent playable male Ogre model.

Implement responsive movement and consistent hand-drawn directional animations.

Create a small attractive wilderness environment.

The model must look and move convincingly at actual gameplay zoom.

## Milestone 2 — The Ogre's Strength

Build the first excellent melee combat encounter.

Implement meaningful impact, light/heavy attacks, blocks, parries, dodges, enemy reactions, and one contextual finisher.

Focus on satisfying gameplay rather than ability count.

## Milestone 3 — Hunt and Feed

Implement one memorable huntable creature.

Allow the player to harvest meat and materials, prepare food, and receive useful benefits.

Introduce a basic feeding finisher.

## Milestone 4 — The First Traveling Hearth

Implement one usable caravan wagon, one temporary camp, a cooking hearth, storage, and camp deployment.

The player can gather resources, upgrade the camp, and move to a new location.

## Milestone 5 — The First Beast Companion

Implement one properly animated animal companion capable of following the player, responding to commands, and performing one useful transportation or hunting task.

## Milestone 6 — The First Clan

Add a few memorable NPCs, useful autonomous labor, and a meaningful local progression system.

The player should begin feeling that he belongs to a living traveling community.

After these milestones, expand into creature breeding, mounted combat, mercenary contracts, larger clan systems, and the narrative opening.

# 17. TESTING AND DELIVERY

All implementation must be tested in Godot 4.

Verify:

- The game imports and launches.
- Input and movement feel responsive.
- Characters and weapons animate correctly.
- Combat hit detection is reliable.
- The player regains control properly after finishers.
- Enemies behave consistently.
- Gathering and hunting provide satisfying feedback.
- Resources can be collected and used.
- Camp systems function correctly.
- Save/load preserves meaningful progress.
- Performance is appropriate for ordinary gameplay.
- Art quality matches the intended direction.

Commit meaningful changes regularly.

Provide playable builds when possible, preferably Windows builds that are easy to download, unzip, and launch.

Verify GitHub pushes, tags, and releases before claiming success.

Do not rely solely on a potentially failing automated publication workflow; diagnose failures and use a supported alternative when necessary.

Maintain clear documentation and checkpoints so future development sessions can resume without repeatedly reconstructing the project's architecture or design history.

# 18. YOUR CREATIVE FREEDOM

I want you to approach this as a game designer, not merely a programmer executing a checklist.

**Be inventive, particularly with combat, hunting, eating, beast handling, and the traveling civilization.**

If you discover an original mechanic that makes these activities more enjoyable, experiment with it.

Examples might include:

- An Ogre throwing a goblin into another enemy.
- Grappling a lesser warrior and using him to disrupt a formation.
- A hunting beast coordinating an attack with its Ogre master.
- An Ogre using overwhelming strength to break through an obstacle.
- A well-timed feeding execution restoring combat momentum.
- Coordinated attacks against the vulnerable parts of a giant monster.
- A warbeast physically breaching a fortified gate.
- A favorite animal learning new commands as its bond with the player develops.
- A great hunt providing enough meat to host a feast that attracts new followers.
- A wandering clan gaining increasingly impressive wagons and mobile workshops as it prospers.

These are examples to explore, not mandates to implement everything immediately.

Find the enjoyable interactions and build outward from them.

Do not sacrifice quality for speed or sheer quantity.

A handful of exceptionally satisfying mechanics is much more valuable than a large number of incomplete ones.

---

# FINAL CREATIVE MANDATE

**Hearth and Hunger is about being an enormous, powerful Ogre in a savage world and gradually building a life, household, and legacy through your own actions.**

The player begins with little.

He may become a hunter, craftsman, beastmaster, mercenary, patriarch, chieftain, or conqueror.

He should enjoy personally chopping timber, hunting dangerous beasts, fighting enemies, cooking great meals, raising animals, and spending time among his people.

His growing clan should become physically more impressive.

His accomplishments should matter.

His people should remember him.

His home should travel alongside him.

And when he eventually commands a great wandering host, the player should still be free to pick up his axe and go hunting.

**Make it fun to be one Ogre before trying to simulate an entire Ogre civilization.**

Begin by auditing the existing project, safely preserving its history, and implementing the new Ogre-centered gameplay direction.

Create real, playable, testable progress and publish verified changes to GitHub.

Build a game that players want to return to for just one more day.
