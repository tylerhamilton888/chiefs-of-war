# Hearth and Hunger — Game Design Bible

*Working design, 25 November 2026. Read [Project state](../../PROJECT_STATE.md) for shipped behavior and [Decisions](../../DECISIONS.md) for provisional details.*

## An Ogre's life

A wagon axle cracks on the morning of a hunt. An Ogre can haul the timber, help his father mend it, then take the same track out of camp to find supper. Years later, his own wagon might carry a bronze cauldron looted from a lord, pulled by a beast he raised and fed. The pleasure of bringing something home connects fighting, eating, exploration and craft.

The game combines the adventurous spirit of Conan, creatures and cultures drawn from European mythology, and the bright material contrasts of classic Warcraft. Follow the Ogre as a hunter, craftsman, independent adventurer, beastmaster, mercenary or head of a household. Kinship, courtship, conquest and sworn service remain choices. A modest wagon and one beloved animal can sustain an entire adventure.

## People and place

An adult Ogre stands roughly ten feet tall. Thick neck, broad chest, substantial legs, long upper canines and immense hands give him a bear's presence. A welcoming cook, an assured hunter and a frightened young soldier should read as individuals. Ogres live indefinitely unless killed and resist supernatural terror. A creature that alarms everyone else might make an Ogre wonder how it tastes. Danger still has consequences.

Nomadic clans keep the remains of an older civilization. Use Norse, Germanic and Celtic influences in timber carving, woven cloth, rough iron, hide, knotwork and harness. Ogres prize practical skill, immediate pleasures, prestige and personal loyalties. A human gate can become a shield, a cannon a personal firearm. Most ambitious fighters seek a profitable employer. Few care to govern an empire.

Human rulers have reasons both to fear an Ogre and to commission his services. Dwarven plate, Elven finery, Orc steel and Goblin contraptions each need recognizable construction, provenance and a use in play. Supernatural foes should command respect. An Ogre cook's solemn preference for well-aged undead meat can carry a scene without making its enemy ridiculous. Treat choices such as the Cairnborn, Hollow Court or insectoid peoples as future opportunities with a concrete adventure attached.

## The traveling household

At the Hearth, inspect equipment, ask a contact about work, choose a meal and decide where to venture. A short outing can secure a carcass, a new blade or safe grazing. A longer contract might recover a smokehouse fitting and reopen a road. On return, use the spoils, trade them, fit them to the wagon or share them. The home, supply routes and people should show the result.

Begin independent travel with a modest wagon and a beast. Acquire shelters, storage, cooking and beast-handling equipment, trophies, companions and family through play. Preserve physical arrangements and possessions when packing and deploying. Let helpers handle routine transport and processing as the household prospers. Holding land, raising adult children who undertake missions, commanding a clan and founding an empire are later elective ambitions.

## Character and combat

Create a male Ogre, name him and his clan, select a background and make him recognizable through face, hair, beard, skin, colors, scars, paint, tattoos and heraldry. Show him moving in the creator. One stable adult body makes grips and armor reliable across directions. Age can distinguish authored NPCs. The playable character progresses visually through what he wears, where he has been and what he carries.

WASD moves. In camp, the Ogre turns with his stride. In combat, the cursor sets his facing while movement can carry him toward, across or away from a threat. Start input immediately and show weight through planted feet, anticipation, follow-through, sound and target reaction. Blocking, parrying, dodging and positioning solve different problems. Normal actions remain available without stamina, hunger or compulsory Ferocity expenditure.

An exposed unarmored goblin comes apart under one clean cleaver hit. The player may eat an eligible piece, gather meat for the camp pot or move on. A group becomes dangerous through spears, crossfire, traps and numbers. Armored foes can brace or deflect. Rival Ogres and great beasts can contest strength directly. A grapple, throw or shove follows a readable opening and the target's size. Break an authored barricade, disrupt a formation, or lift something heavy enough to change the route. Resolve contact in the same system in campaign and arena. Keep violence brief, forceful and stylized.

Build around complete weapons and meaningful variations. Cleavers, heavy clubs, polearms, bows and repurposed firearms should change reach, rhythm, recoil, grip or reaction. Each supported weapon works at baseline. Gear from different peoples can be mixed, while matching sets confer coherent visual and mechanical benefits. Prove a second weapon and two compatible sets in motion before expanding the catalogue.

## Food, plunder and permanent development

Earn ordinary experience and loot through adventures. Separately, eating dishes and using different preparation methods awards distinct kinds of dietary experience. Spend these resources on permanent talent unlocks. Equip a limited selection of active and passive talents and change builds conveniently. Show what a known dish awards and what the player can unlock. Diet categories need text or icons as well as color. An exceptional troll preparation could unlock regeneration. Learning every defeated creature's powers would undermine the value of a remarkable discovery.

A hunt produces recognizable meat, organs, hides and occasional unusual ingredients. Common spoils collect conveniently. A cut can feed the Ogre, fill the pot, preserve a journey, bait prey, suit a particular animal, settle a bargain or earn hospitality. One ingredient spent on supper is gone from the pack. A recipe learned from an elder remains known when supplies run out. Give players recipe knowledge through people, commissions, heirlooms or finds. Show the needed ingredients and work. Let them repeat a learned cut, stir, roast or preserve action with increasingly good equipment and help.

Meals can also recover health or grant a lasting outing benefit. Menus and conversations should leave the player free to linger. Tune ordinary hunger so life at the Hearth gives preparation a purpose without disabling the Ogre's basic kit. Reward varied fighting builds with useful spoils. Bloodhearts, spirit-bound marrow, demon flesh or mineral food for a stone-eater are possible discoveries. Their names, effects, repeat-use rules and costs remain open.

Pay can include food, livestock, equipment, information or access. A harness or cooking vessel is tangible plunder with a place at home. An Ogre can carry a great object himself, set it down quickly in a fight, and later have a beast or helper haul it back. A completed job grants its unique reward once across travel and reloads.

## Beasts, travel and allegiances

Begin with one animal whose gait, appetite, tasks and responses make it memorable. A working beast pulls or hauls. A hunting partner can track or flush prey. A battlefield partner might create an opening. Let the player protect, feed, handle and harness a companion over time. Food comes from elsewhere. A Stonewood Bull can be saved or befriended. Routine defeat allows recovery for the animal and ordinary adventuring companions. Training, breeding and beastmaster builds can grow after that first animal works.

Use 2D at both scales. Local scenes have collision, depth sorting, ground shadows and traversable space around substantial objects. The eventual world map is a living illustrated landscape with free movement, readable terrain and moving caravans, patrols, herds and armies. Show the player's actual Hearth and give the journey decisions of its own. Authored landmarks and adventures provide continuity. Limited simulation can change patrols, prices, access and opportunities. Significant events should persist across visits.

Mercenary work ranges from a one-off job to campaigning with a ruler. Make obligations, promised provisions, rights, payment and likely risks clear. Desertion, betrayal, rescue or reliable work matter to particular employers, settlements and companions. Equipment commissions display the culture of the maker. Track who witnessed or learned of an act and which relationships it affected. Give the player room to change sides or remain independent.

## Presentation and evidence

Use rich Classic Fantasy colors, clear silhouettes and warm or cold light appropriate to the place. Mood paintings can carry fine detail. An axe, a face, a wagon doorway and a goblin spear each need to read at the camera distance where the player uses them. Ground and light independent, interactive scenery consistently. Let the same environment kit make several distinct places that people can actually walk through. The seven workshop illustrations establish specific qualities. Their camera distances, frame counts and printed statistics are provisional. [Art & Animation Bible](../art/ART_AND_ANIMATION_BIBLE.md)

Use the actual character in ordinary camp and family scenes. Detailed NPC portraits and illustrated events may carry major moments. If a painting would show the wrong face, equipment, companions or wagon, stage the event in engine or frame the place from a viewpoint that preserves continuity. Make controls, objectives, known recipes, equipment comparisons and contract terms legible without needing a designer to explain them.

Start at Emberhide Crossing. Judge character creation, walking, first equipment and the first household job at actual gameplay zoom. Keep the arena as a quick way to compare poses, loadouts, hits and resistant targets with disposable state. Document what a normal-speed clip or build really demonstrates and what remains open to player review. [Opening outline](OPENING_OUTLINE.md) · [Development order](../ROADMAP.md)

Author definitions under stable IDs, keep field rewards and inventory instances distinct, and preserve versioned campaign saves. Store art masters, runtime sheets and review copies separately. After changes to an atlas, check its pivots, alpha, grips and scale in motion. The build guide records the enforced asset and preview budgets. [Build guide](../BUILD.md)
