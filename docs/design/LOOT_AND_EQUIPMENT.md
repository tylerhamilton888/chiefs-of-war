# Loot and equipment

Treasure hunting is a pillar of Hearth and Hunger. A good expedition may bring home a helmet worth wearing, supper for the family, or enough valuables to buy a wagon improvement. A run can be rewarding even when it yields no equipment.

## Item identity and progression

Author base types with recognizable silhouettes, weapon rhythms, slots and modest intrinsic stats. An instance keeps its base, quality roll, named modifier and value, rarity, level requirement and optional legendary or set identity. Roll once when a reward is claimed and save the result. Show rarity by text and color. Make significant legendary items recognizable by name and artwork. Give set pieces a common visual motif, show the number worn, and show the bonus at each threshold.

Equipment should have a useful effect as soon as an eligible Ogre wears it. Comparisons must account for the outgoing item, weapon speed and any set bonus gained or lost. Use small numbers and deliberate level gates so a good older find can earn its place. The opening requires no tempering, sockets, enchantments or rerolls. Reuse fitted equipment illustrations across ordinary modifier rolls. Reserve distinct art for named treasures and sets.

## How treasure enters the world

Keep an open random pool. Also give particular exceptional items identifiable routes through hunts, contracts, quests, commissions and exploration. Tell the player what a reliable route pays before they commit. A targeted route can award the item directly or give concrete progress toward it. It should have a finish line independent of a lucky roll.

Ingredients, valuables, trophies, recipes, furnishings, beast equipment, materials and artifacts all have a place in reward tables. Use abundant useful supplies and fewer pieces of gear. Tie drops to their source. A hunter's kit at a wolf kill can contain a helm. The wolf supplies the hide and meat. On repeat dungeon visits, change inhabitants as territory shifts, let exploration reveal previously unknown locations, and assemble some layouts from authored sections with valid entrances, exits and encounter spaces. Keep important authored places recognizable. Procedural dungeons belong after the opening loot loop has proved itself.

## Bringing things home

Use expandable inventory and Hearth storage slots, generous stacks, and quick sorting. Carrying capacity is measured in slots. Give consumables and common materials convenient stacks and keep the coin purse accessible. A full pack preserves the find so the player can return for it. Possessions can serve as merchandise, salvage, gifts, displays, stockpiles and eventually religious offerings. Each use transfers or consumes the same owned possession once. Cooking and sharing meals already give an example of that principle.

## First playable pool

| Opportunity | Current reward |
| --- | --- |
| First harvested wolf in the Pines or Hollow | Six cuts, two hides, trade amber and a level-one wearable from a torn hunter's pack. The base yields immediate protection and has a random quality and modifier. |
| Later wolves and hostiles | Seedable weighted rolls over road helm, ring mantle, trail axe and quarry maul. Most ordinary encounters yield no loose gear. The Hollow alpha also yields the feast trophy and a guaranteed gear roll. |
| Cairnwatch pieces | Helm and mantle bear the same three-stone badge. Wearing both adds two points of armor beyond their individual stats. |
| Crown of the Red Hart | Can roll randomly. Eydis also promises it for the three-raider Cairn contract. Completion qualifies the Ogre to wear it. Its antler plate and strike bonus identify it. |
| Return to camp | Meat for the feast, amber to trade, hides for pack slots and the wagon chest for stockpiles. The chest supports depositing, retrieving, selling and salvaging gear. |

The opening uses six pack gear slots and sixteen chest gear slots, with an expansion for each. Equipped items have their own slots. Common supplies stack separately in the pack and wagon. The first three adventure levels are independent of dietary advancement. Selling at the chest stands in for household trade. General gifting, display furniture, further targeted routes, new loot categories and repeatable dungeon generation remain later work.

## Playtest questions

Can a first-time player find the comparison from the harvest message, explain their choice, and see the change on their Ogre? Does returning with meat, amber and hides feel worthwhile when they pass on a weapon? Over several trips, record equips, items sold or salvaged, repeat finds and full-pack events. Compare time to a desired legendary through luck and through its named source. Tune drop chances, affix ranges, XP thresholds, slot counts, upgrade costs and sell values from those sessions.
